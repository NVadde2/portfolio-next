Navigating the tech job market can feel like a full-time job in itself. Between LeetCode grinds, resume tweaks, and system design diagrams, it's easy to feel overwhelmed. Gear up! I'm about to bombard you with a lot of info that will help you build momentum, master the interview, and maintain your sanity.

Hey there! I've been where you are — navigating the tricky waters of job interviews, coding assessments, and preparing for a career in tech. It all started for me back in 2020, when I was fresh out of my bachelor's degree and knee-deep in competitive programming. Back then, **Hackerrank** was my go-to platform, **GeeksforGeeks** was my lifeline for DSA topics, and **Stack Overflow** was my savior whenever I hit an error I couldn't figure out.

Now, it's 2024, and let me tell you — things have changed. Leetcode has taken over as the holy grail of coding interview prep. And with all the research, trial, and error I've been through over the years, I'm here to share what worked best for me and what might just work for you, too. So let's dive right into it.

## Master the Technical Fundamentals

Before you start applying, you need a solid foundation. Don't try to learn everything — focus on the patterns that actually appear in interviews.

- **The Big Four:** Arrays/Strings, Trees/Graphs, Dynamic Programming, and Linked Lists.
- **The 'Blind 75' Strategy:** Start with the **Blind 75 on NeetCode**. It's a curated list that covers the most important patterns. NeetCode's video explanations are a lifesaver for visual learners.
- **System Design:** For mid-level roles and above, read *Grokking the System Design Interview*. Practice building scalable architectures like URL shorteners or chat apps.
- **The Bible:** *Cracking the Coding Interview* by Gayle Laakmann McDowell remains the gold standard for understanding what top-tier tech companies expect.

## Optimize Your "First Impression" Tools

One thing I learned at countless career fairs is that your resume and LinkedIn are your marketing brochures.

- **Iterative Resumes:** Don't wait until you feel "100% ready" to apply. Update your resume after every few interviews based on the feedback or gaps you notice.
- **Leverage LinkedIn Like a Pro:** Trust me on this. LinkedIn is hands down the best platform to form connections with industry professionals. Follow important people in tech, engage with their content, and take note of the advice they share. You'd be surprised how much wisdom is out there if you know where to look.

## Bonus Tips: What I've Learned from the Interview Trenches

To wrap this up, here's a collection of the specific habits and mindsets that made all the difference in my prep:

- **Don't skip the basics:** Make sure you're comfortable with data structures and algorithms (yes, the dreaded DSA). Even though it seems like the basics, mastering these concepts will make harder problems more manageable.
- **Mock interviews:** Find a friend or use a platform like Pramp or Interviewing.io to practice real-time interviews. The more you practice speaking your thought process out loud, the more comfortable you'll be in actual interviews.
- **Explain your thought process:** When you're solving coding problems in an interview, remember to explain why you're doing what you're doing. Interviewers want to know how you approach a problem, not just that you can solve it.
- **Pro Tip:** Check out this incredibly useful [coding interview checklist](https://www.faangshui.com/) that I found from FAANGShui.
- **Keep a job tracker:** Use a simple spreadsheet to track the jobs you've applied to, including the status of each application. Create a dashboard that gives you insights on where to improve — whether it's your resume, interview skills, or follow-ups.
- **Stay consistent:** The key to improving is consistency. Make coding practice a habit. Even just dedicating an hour each day can lead to significant improvement over time.
- **Schedule focused study time:** Use a timer to set aside blocks of uninterrupted study time (like the Pomodoro technique). Focused work sessions with regular breaks help you avoid burnout and improve retention.
- **Spaced repetition:** Don't just cram. Use the spaced repetition technique to review and solidify concepts over time. This helps you retain what you've learned and master the topics.
- **Don't give up:** The interview process can be tough, and you may face rejection. But don't let that discourage you. Keep pushing through — it's part of the journey.
- **Behavioral interviews matter:** Don't just focus on coding. Many companies place a lot of emphasis on behavioral interviews (Google, Amazon, looking at you). Be prepared to talk about your past projects, how you've handled challenges, and how you work in a team.

## Final Thoughts

Interview prep is a journey, and everyone's path is different. The key is to find a system that works for you and stick with it. If you ever feel stuck or overwhelmed, remember — you're not alone. Consistency beats intensity every time. Whether it's one LeetCode problem a day or one networking message a week, small actions compound. Your job search is a marathon, not a sprint — take care of your mental health along the way. Good luck out there!
